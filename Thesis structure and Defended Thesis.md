Thesis structure and Defended Thesis
Your thesis follows this structure: introduction with all formal elements, four chapters each ending with a summary, conclusions, literature, appendices. Page counts are guides, not rules.
Part	Content for your topic	Pages
Front matter	Title pages LV/EN, abstract LV/EN, keywords, contents, lists of figures and tables	—
Introduction	Topicality, aim, object, subject, research problem, tasks, hypotheses (copy from your application), methods, description of the prototype, practical value, approbation, defended thesis, structure	5–7
1 Literature review and theoretical background	SAP evolution ECC → S/4HANA → RISE → Embrace; hyperscaler models; cloud provider selection studies; AHP, TOPSIS; Design Science Research; table comparing existing approaches (vendor calculators, analyst reports, academic MCDA models) showing the gap	12–18
2 Case and system analysis	Decision context; stakeholders (CIO, SAP architect, procurement); functional requirements (inputs, 4 output screens); non-functional (every number traceable to a dated source, reproducible); constraints and risks, including how RISE bundles infrastructure cost	6–10
3 Development and experiment	Three-layer architecture; data layer (normalisation.csv, prices.csv, criteria.csv); cost engine formulas; AHP with CR; TOPSIS; results for reference profile(s); back-test on cases; sensitivity and switch-points; test of H1 and H2	15–20
4 Discussion and development	Mismatch analysis (why companies chose differently from the tool); comparison with one vendor calculator; limitations; future improvements	5–8
Conclusions	One answer per task, H1/H2 verdict, defended thesis confirmed	2–3
Appendices	Link to the GitHub repository and the GitHub Pages prototype, full normalisation table, prices.csv with sources and snapshot date, AHP matrices, case profiles	—
Defended Thesis (draft — refine after phase 6):
1.Has been offered a three-layer decision-support prototype that combines a range-based, source-referenced cost model with multi-criteria analysis for comparing the AWS, Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud pathways under SAP Embrace.
2.Has been modified the AHP-based multi-criteria selection method for the hyperscaler selection task, by adding a service normalisation table that maps differently named and differently measured services of the three providers to common comparison units.
3.Has been suggested a validation methodology for such tools that combines back-testing against documented enterprise migrations, mismatch analysis and sensitivity switch-points.
